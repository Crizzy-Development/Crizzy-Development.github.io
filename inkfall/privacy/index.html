Inkfall Privacy Policy

This policy applies to the Inkfall app for Android and iOS, published by an independent developer. Crizzy Development is the name the apps on this site are published under; it is not a separate legal entity.

Effective October 29, 2026.

The short version. Your stories, meaning the worlds you choose, what you write and everything the app records while you play, are stored on your device. There is no account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. We collect no personal information, and nothing about you is sold or shared.

One thing leaves your device as you play: to write each passage, Inkfall sends what you wrote, together with the story so far and the state the app keeps for it, to Google's Gemini API, an AI service that returns the next passage. It goes from your device to Google directly, as our service provider. It does not pass through any system of ours, and we never see it. The section on the narrator below says exactly what is sent and what Google does with it.

One more thing can leave, and only if you send it: a report of a passage the narrator should not have written. Inkfall writes the report as an email in your own mail app, and nothing reaches us until you send that email. The section on reporting a passage says what a report contains.

Information we collect

None, unless you write to us. Inkfall has no user accounts, no sign-in and no registration. We do not collect, store, sell or share any personal information, and the app gives us no way to identify you. The narrator's requests go from your device to Google and back without touching anything of ours, so we hold no copy of your stories and could not read one.

If you send us a report of a passage, or any other email, we receive what you sent, including the email address it was sent from. The section on reporting a passage says exactly what a report contains and what we do with it.

Inkfall contains no advertising software, no analytics and no third-party tracking software. In fact, significant efforts have been made to explicitly avoid such capabilities.

Information stored only on your device

The app stores the things you put into it and the things it records as you play:

  • The worlds you choose and the stories you begin.
  • What you write at each turn, and the passages the narrator returns.
  • The state of each story: what you carry, the pressures building around you, the conditions you are under, and what has come to light.
  • Which Codex entries you have seen, whether you asked not to see the intro again, and your settings.
  • The passages you have reported: which passage, the reasons you chose, your note, and when you handed the report to your mail app, so the passage stays marked and you can send it again.
  • A ledger of the narrator's calls: which turn, which provider and model, how much text went back and forth, and whether the result was used. Identifiers and numbers only, never story text.

All of it is held in the app's private storage on your device. It is not transmitted to us and it is not accessible to us.

Deleting a story removes its text, its state and the record of its reports. The call ledger keeps its rows, because they describe what this installation of the app has spent rather than any one story, and they contain no story text. Uninstalling Inkfall removes all of it, subject to the backup section below.

The app can copy that ledger to your clipboard as a table, from the Game State Inspector, when you ask it to. It goes only where you paste it.

The narrator

A narrator writes the passage that follows what you do. It is an AI model, Google's Gemini, which the app reaches through Google's Gemini API. The rules of the world are not the model's to change: the app decides what your actions cost and what becomes true, and the model writes the prose.

Each turn, the app sends Google:

  • what you wrote this turn;
  • the passages and actions so far, or as many recent ones as fit;
  • the parts of your story's state the narrator is allowed to know: what you carry, the pressures in play, what has come to light, and the world's own rules for how it is written.

It never sends anything about you. No name, no account, no identifier of yours, and nothing from elsewhere on your device. Google receives the request from your device, so it sees your device's internet address, as any website you visit does.

What is sent is governed by Google's terms for the Gemini API's paid services. Under those terms, as they stand at the date above, Google does not use it to train or improve its models, and may hold it for a limited time to detect abuse. The passage that comes back is stored on your device like everything else, and no copy of the exchange is kept by us.

The iOS test build does not yet have this narrator: its passages come from text built into the app, and it sends nothing. It gains the live narrator in a later version.

Reporting a passage

Any passage the narrator writes can be reported: tap the flag beneath it, or press and hold the passage. You choose what is wrong with it, from offensive or hateful content to content beyond the bounds of the story or harmful advice, and you can add a note.

Inkfall has no server for reports. Send by email opens a new email in your mail app, addressed to crizzydevelopment@gmail.com, with the report already written. Nothing is sent until you send that email from your own account, and you can read every word of it first: in the app, under "What this report carries", and in the email itself.

A report contains:

  • the passage you reported, and the three turns before it: what you wrote and what the narrator returned;
  • the reasons you chose and your note;
  • details of the story: the world it is set in, the story's identifier in the app, the turn, when the passage was written, which AI provider and model wrote it, and the app's version with your device's operating system and its version;
  • the parts of the story's state the narrator was given when it wrote the passage, named by the codes the app's own content uses rather than in words;
  • a check value at the end, which lets us tell whether the report below the "Do Not Edit" line was changed.

Nothing else from your device is included. Because it is an email, it also carries what any email carries: the address you send it from, and the name your mail account shows.

We use a report to understand what the narrator wrote and why, to tighten the bounds it writes within, and to reply to you. We do not sell or share them, and we keep each one only as long as we need it for those purposes. To have a report you sent deleted, write to the same address.

If your device has no mail app set up, Inkfall offers to copy the report instead, so you can paste it into an email from anywhere. Copying sends nothing.

When Inkfall uses the network

When the narrator writes, and at no other time. Each turn is one request from your device to Google's servers. Nothing else in the app uses the network: no analytics, no crash reports, no checks for updates. A report does not use Inkfall's connection: the app hands the email to your mail app, which sends it if you do. Without a connection a new passage cannot be written, and everything already in your story can still be read.

Backup to your own cloud account

Your device's own backup may copy the app's data to your personal cloud account, so that your stories return when you set up a new device. This is a feature of the operating system, not of Inkfall. The data goes to your account, not to us, and your platform's terms and privacy policy govern it.

  • On Android, this is Auto Backup to your Google account.
  • On iOS, this is iCloud Backup.

Either can be turned off in your device settings. Neither is controlled by Inkfall.

Crash reports

Inkfall contains no crash-reporting software.

If you have turned on your platform's own diagnostics sharing ("usage and diagnostics" on Android, "share with app developers" on iOS), your platform may make anonymous, aggregated crash and performance information available to us through Google Play or App Store Connect. That reporting is a function of the operating system and the store, is controlled by your device settings, and does not identify you to us.

Children

Inkfall is intended for players 16 and over, and its store ratings say so. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included.

Your choices

We hold no information about you unless you have written to us, and then only what you sent. Sending a report is always your choice, and a report you sent can be deleted on request. Everything the app holds is on your device and under your control: delete a story from the Home screen (hold to delete, and it is not reversible), or uninstall the app to remove all of it. To remove a copy held by your platform's backup, use your Google or iCloud account's backup settings.

What has been sent to the narrator cannot be recalled from Google by us or by you; its retention is governed by Google's terms above. Not taking a turn is the only way to send nothing, and everything already in a story can be read without one.
